Task:
Develop a puzzle game where the player must group 16 words into four sets of four, based on hidden categories. The words are presented in a 4x4 grid, and players select four at a time to form a group. Correct groups are removed and revealed with a category and a color-coded difficulty (yellow, green, blue, purple). Incorrect guesses count as mistakes, with a maximum of four allowed. Only one correct solution exists, though words may appear to fit multiple categories. Include shuffle functionality and provide immediate feedback after each guess. A new puzzle is generated daily.

Modality:
as the chief product officer, i've analyzed the customer's requirements for this word-grouping puzzle game let me share my thoughts on the product modality:

**my recommendation: website (html)**

here's my reasoning:

1 **interactivity is core**: the game requires players to click/select words in a 4x4 grid, submit guesses, and receive immediate feedback a website built with html/css/javascript handles this click-driven interaction natively and smoothly

2 **accessibility & daily usage**: since a new puzzle is generated daily, players need frictionless access a website requires no installation—users simply open it in any browser, which perfectly suits the "daily puzzle" habit loop

3 **visual design requirements**: the color-coded difficulty system (yellow, green, blue, purple), category reveal animations, and shuffle functionality are all naturally implemented with css/javascript, giving us full control over the visual polish

4 **daily puzzle generation**: javascript can generate or rotate puzzles based on the date, making the daily refresh seamless without any backend dependency

the alternative would be an **application (python)**, which could also implement the game logic, but it would require users to have python installed, adding friction for a casual daily puzzle audience

what are your thoughts? do you agree with the website modality, or do you prefer another option?
